Folders → Labels

Same structure. Different name.

Gmail uses labels instead of folders. Your Outlook organization is preserved — but there's one mental shift to make.

In Outlook: a folder is a place. An email lives in one folder. To organize, you move it.

In Gmail: a label is a tag. An email can have multiple labels at once. Labels don't move emails — they tag them.

Practical effect after migration: every old Outlook folder is now a Gmail label in your left sidebar. Click a label to see only emails tagged with it.

Preserved. Subfolders become nested labels. So if you had Projects/Q1 2026 in Outlook, you'll see it in Gmail's sidebar as:

Projects └─ Q1 2026 └─ Q4 2025 Clients └─ Acme Corp

Click any nested label to see only those emails.

Because labels don't move emails — they tag them. After migration, your old emails are tagged with their label AND still appear in your Inbox by default.

Two ways to clean up:

  1. Archive: Select emails in the Inbox → click the archive button (down-arrow icon). They leave the Inbox but stay accessible under their label.
  2. Move to label: Select emails → click Labels → choose a label to move them. They'll now only show up in that label view.
Automation

Recreate your Outlook rules.

Outlook rules and email forwarding don't automatically migrate. Here's how to rebuild them in Gmail.

Step 1: Open Gmail → click the search box at the top

Step 2: Enter your search criteria (e.g., from:john@example.com, subject:invoice, or has:attachment)

Step 3: Click the dropdown arrow at the far right of the search box, then click "Create filter"

Step 4: Choose actions (apply label, auto-archive, mark as read, forward, etc.)

Step 5: Click "Create filter"

Step 1: Click the gear icon (Settings) in Gmail → Select "See all settings"

Step 2: Go to the "Forwarding and POP/IMAP" tab

Step 3: Click "Add a forwarding address" and enter the recipient's email

Step 4: Confirm from the email Google sends to that address

Step 5: Select "Forward a copy of incoming mail" and choose whether to keep a copy in Gmail

Step 6: Click "Save changes"

Gmail filters are applied in the order you create them for each incoming email. If an email matches multiple filters, all matching actions will be applied.

Unlike Outlook, there's no "stop processing" option, so be careful with conflicting filters. For complex rules, consider using Gmail's search operators and labels to organize instead.
